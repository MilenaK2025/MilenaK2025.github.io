Winter in New York

Eventually, it is time to wear fuzzy coats with oversized hoods, gloves, and boots: it’s winter, and New York is suddenly buzzing with anticipation for the upcoming new year. The truth is, winter in New York is wonderful. Reports on Mariah Carey “defrosting” start all the way back in September, but you never get tired of hearing “All I want for Christmas is You.” As the holidays draw nearer, a beautiful, majestic fir tree is heaved onto a platform at Rockefeller Center, bedazzled like a priceless jewel. You find within yourself that you do, in fact, want to go to Starbucks and try out their new concoction. Snow, which seldom falls, causes you to stop in your tracks; take in a sharp breath; and just watch in silent bliss, ignoring the people who jostle around you. This culture, made up of these intricate details, flourishes into this energy within New York that brings warmth and comfort even when the weather nears its worst with violent wind storms, cold rains, and icy frosts. Those struggling in the cold, or those on their last reserve of motivation, instead look forward to buying a Christmas tree; meeting with relatives and deepening connections; and cooking delicious food with loved ones. In winter, you find more people smiling on the streets, and you find that you, too, want to wave and beam as big as you can. This energy spreads warmth throughout your heart and allows you to step off the speeding highway that is life, even if just for a moment. Winter in New York is wonderful, it really is.
